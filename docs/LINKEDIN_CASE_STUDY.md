# LinkedIn Case-Study Draft

I built a full Microsoft DCF and business-quality model to answer a more useful question than "What is Microsoft worth?"

**What must be true for Microsoft's current valuation to be justified, especially given the scale of its AI and cloud investment?**

The project includes:

- FY2021A-FY2026A historical financial and cash-flow analysis
- Recast segment economics for Productivity & Business Processes, Intelligent Cloud, and More Personal Computing
- Cash conversion, reinvestment intensity, ROIC, and incremental ROIC
- A segment-driven FY2027E-FY2036E forecast
- Cash-Based and Lease-Adjusted UFCF
- WACC and terminal-value analysis
- A reverse DCF and sensitivity framework
- A Buffett-inspired five-tenet business-quality and margin-of-safety scorecard
- Automated validation checks, an executive dashboard, and a final investment memo

A key modeling choice was to include finance-lease additions in Total CapEx. Looking only at cash CapEx would understate the economic scale of Microsoft's AI/datacenter reinvestment.

**Base-case result:** $367.85 per share versus a $499.86 reference price.  
**Reverse DCF result:** the market price requires roughly 7.41 percentage points of additional FY2027E consolidated revenue growth above my base case, fading to zero by FY2036E, while holding margins and reinvestment assumptions constant.  
**Buffett-inspired result:** 88.1 / 100 business quality excluding valuation, but a **WATCH / WAIT** stance because the current price offers no margin of safety.

The biggest takeaway was not a single price target. It was the tension between Microsoft's exceptional franchise quality and the increasingly demanding growth expectations embedded in the valuation.

This is an educational portfolio project, not investment advice.
