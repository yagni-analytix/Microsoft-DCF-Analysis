# Microsoft Investment Memo

## Buffett-Inspired Business Quality and DCF Assessment

**Author:** Yagni Patel  
**Valuation date:** August 6, 2026  
**Reference price:** $499.86  
**Base intrinsic value:** $367.85  
**Stance:** **WATCH / WAIT**  
**Project type:** Educational portfolio analysis; not investment advice

## Executive conclusion

Microsoft is an exceptional business with a broad moat, recurring enterprise revenue, high margins, strong cash conversion, and attractive returns on capital. The investment question is not whether Microsoft is a high-quality company. It is whether the current price provides a sufficient margin of safety after accounting for the extraordinary capital required to build AI and cloud capacity.

The base DCF values Microsoft at **$367.85 per share**, implying **26.41% downside** to the $499.86 reference price. The reverse DCF indicates that the market price requires approximately **7.41 percentage points** of additional FY2027E consolidated revenue growth above the base case, taking implied growth from **16.22% to 23.62%**, with the uplift fading to zero by FY2036E.

The Buffett-inspired framework scores Microsoft **88.1 / 100 on business quality excluding valuation**, but only **74.5 / 100 overall** because the market price offers no margin of safety. The appropriate stance is **WATCH / WAIT**, with a 15% margin-of-safety reference price of approximately **$312.67**.

## Investment question

> Can Microsoft's AI and cloud investments generate sufficient long-term free cash flow growth and returns to justify its current valuation?

The model's answer is: **Microsoft's AI and cloud investments may justify today's valuation, but the current price already assumes a more aggressive monetization path than the base case.**

## Business overview and operating momentum

Microsoft generated FY2026 revenue of **$331.8 billion**, up 18%, and operating income of **$155.2 billion**, up 21%. Azure annual revenue surpassed $100 billion, Microsoft 365 Copilot exceeded 30 million paid seats, and commercial remaining performance obligation reached $678 billion. These results support the durability of the enterprise franchise and the breadth of AI demand.

FY2026 segment results were:

| Segment | Revenue ($mm) | Growth | Operating margin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Productivity & Business Processes | 139,996 | 15.88% | 59.92% |
| Intelligent Cloud | 137,791 | 29.67% | 41.35% |
| More Personal Computing | 54,052 | (1.09%) | 26.62% |

Productivity & Business Processes remains the principal margin engine. Intelligent Cloud is the main growth engine, but its economics are more capital-intensive and exposed to utilization, depreciation, energy, and AI-serving costs.

## Business quality and owner economics

Microsoft's FY2026 operating margin reached **46.78%**, while CFO was **136.77% of net income**. These metrics support the quality of reported operating earnings. ROIC was **33.55%**, although it declined from prior years as invested capital expanded.

The most important trend is the divergence between accounting profitability and cash economics:

- Cash FCF margin declined to **20.19%**.
- Total CapEx / Revenue reached **42.36%** after including finance-lease additions.
- Lease-adjusted UFCF margin fell to **9.32%**.
- Incremental ROIC was **19.34%**, above the **9.47% WACC**, but below Microsoft's historical returns.

This does not imply that AI investment is destroying value. It implies that the proof period is still underway. Microsoft must convert installed capacity into durable revenue and profit while allowing CapEx intensity to normalize.

## Forecast and valuation

The base forecast models each segment independently through FY2036E. Consolidated revenue growth declines from **16.22% in FY2027E** toward **4.74% in FY2036E**. Operating margin remains between roughly 46% and 47%, while Total CapEx / Revenue declines from 43% to 16% as the infrastructure cycle normalizes.

The DCF uses lease-adjusted UFCF, a mid-year convention, a **9.47% WACC**, and a **3.00% terminal growth rate**.

| Valuation output | Result |
|---|---:|
| Enterprise value | $2,735,267 million |
| Equity value | $2,741,570 million |
| Intrinsic value per share | $367.85 |
| Current market price | $499.86 |
| Implied downside | (26.41%) |
| Terminal value / enterprise value | 65.90% |
| Implied terminal EV/UFCF | 15.92x |

The sensitivity range is approximately **$286.80 to $519.92 per share** across the selected WACC and terminal-growth cases. The current price is only supported near the optimistic end of that valuation range.

## Reverse DCF

The reverse DCF holds operating margins, reinvestment, WACC, and terminal growth constant and solves for the revenue trajectory required to reproduce the market price.

| Metric | Base case | Market implied |
|---|---:|---:|
| FY2027E revenue growth | 16.22% | 23.62% |
| Growth uplift | - | 7.41 pp |
| Enterprise value | $2.735 trillion | $3.719 trillion |
| Implied share price | $367.85 | $499.86 |

The reverse DCF is demanding because the growth uplift remains material through much of the explicit forecast before fading to zero in FY2036E. In practical terms, the market is assuming faster Azure and AI monetization without a corresponding deterioration in margins or reinvestment efficiency.

## Catalysts

- Azure growth remains near or above 40% and capacity constraints ease.
- Microsoft 365 Copilot paid seats convert into meaningful ARPU expansion.
- Intelligent Cloud operating margin stabilizes and begins to recover.
- Total CapEx intensity declines faster than modeled.
- Incremental ROIC remains above 15% as invested capital scales.
- The share price declines toward or below the margin-of-safety range.

## Principal risks

- AI and datacenter investment remains elevated longer than expected.
- Capacity is underutilized or AI workload pricing becomes structurally less attractive.
- Competition compresses Azure, Copilot, or developer-platform economics.
- Security, privacy, regulatory, or antitrust events weaken the ecosystem.
- The terminal valuation remains highly sensitive to WACC and perpetual growth.
- The reverse DCF framework solves for one variable; other combinations of growth, margins, and reinvestment could also justify the market price.

## Monitoring framework

The thesis should be reassessed each quarter against five indicators:

1. Consolidated revenue growth versus the 23.62% market-implied FY2027E threshold
2. Intelligent Cloud operating margin and Azure growth
3. Copilot paid seats, pricing, and reported monetization
4. Total CapEx / Revenue and lease-adjusted UFCF recovery
5. Incremental ROIC relative to WACC

## Final recommendation

**WATCH / WAIT.** Microsoft is one of the highest-quality large-cap businesses in public markets, but quality and price must be evaluated separately. At $499.86, the base model provides no margin of safety and requires a more aggressive AI/cloud growth path than the operating forecast.
